# Wise Leadership Team — Job Descriptions and Compensation

Four-person executive team for a ~$50M commercial printing operation with five plants (business forms, labels, packaging).

**Structure:** CEO, with VP of Sales, VP of Operations, and VP of Finance reporting directly. Five Plant General Managers report to the VP of Operations.

---

## Scope Boundaries

Three functions cause most org-chart arguments in printing. Settled here up front:

| Function | Owner | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Customer service / order entry | **VP Operations** | CSRs sit at the plants, next to scheduling. One function, common standards. Industry norm in forms and labels. |
| Estimating (cost) | **VP Operations** | An estimate is a manufacturing calculation. |
| Pricing (price to market) | **VP Sales** | Within margin floors set by Finance. |
| Margin policy / price-list governance | **VP Finance** | Maintains cost model and floors; reports realization. |
| Pricing exceptions beyond policy | **CEO** | Defined threshold, not case-by-case negotiation. |

Nobody is paid on a measure they don't control. Every role carries some EBITDA exposure so they row together.

---

## CEO

**Purpose:** Own enterprise results and decide where Wise puts its capital and its capacity.

**Reports to:** Board / ownership
**Direct reports:** VP Sales, VP Operations, VP Finance, HR lead

**Owns**
- Enterprise P&L, strategy, and annual operating plan
- Capital allocation across five plants and the label/packaging build
- Plant footprint decisions — specialization, consolidation, closure
- M&A: sourcing, diligence, and integration of tuck-in acquisitions
- Executive team selection, development, and accountability
- Board and lender relationships
- Pricing exceptions above delegated authority

**Measures**
- EBITDA vs. plan
- Revenue growth, with label and packaging broken out
- Cash conversion / return on invested capital
- Integration milestones
- Leadership bench depth and succession coverage

---

## VP of Sales

**Purpose:** Grow revenue and margin, and shift the mix toward label and packaging.

**Reports to:** CEO
**Direct reports:** Outside sales, national/channel accounts, marketing, sales operations

**Owns**
- Revenue vs. plan and the sales plan by segment
- Channel strategy — distributor/trade vs. direct, and the mix between them
- Sales force: hiring, territories, quotas, development, performance management
- Pricing to market within approved margin floors
- Label and packaging revenue development, working against Operations' capability roadmap
- Marketing, samples, trade shows, and the CRM/pipeline system
- Customer retention and win-back on the repeat-revenue base

**Does not own**
- Customer service and order entry (Operations)
- Estimating (Operations)
- Margin floors (Finance)

**Measures**
- Revenue vs. plan
- Gross margin dollars and price realization vs. floor
- Label and packaging revenue growth vs. plan
- New account acquisition and customer retention
- Rep productivity and quota attainment

---

## VP of Operations

**Purpose:** Run five plants as one manufacturing system, and build the capacity to absorb label and packaging growth.

**Reports to:** CEO
**Direct reports:** Five Plant General Managers, customer service, purchasing, logistics, prepress, quality/safety

**Owns**
- Manufacturing performance across all five plants, led through the GMs
- One Wise manufacturing system: standard work, daily management, Lean deployment, problem solving
- Customer service and order entry — quote and order turnaround, order accuracy, service responsiveness
- Estimating, scheduling, and job release
- Purchasing, supply chain, and inbound/outbound logistics
- Prepress, quality, and safety
- Capital equipment: selection, justification, installation, startup, validation
- Label and packaging manufacturing capability roadmap and which plant makes what
- GM development, succession, and the Anderson GM hire

**Does not own**
- Pricing to market (Sales)
- Margin policy (Finance)

**Measures**
- Revenue per manufacturing day / throughput vs. baseline
- Manufacturing lead time and on-time delivery
- Quote and order turnaround; order accuracy
- Makeready time and labor productivity
- Waste, scrap, and quality
- Recordable safety incidents
- Label/packaging capacity and capability milestones on schedule
- GM performance and bench depth

---

## VP of Finance

**Purpose:** Close the books fast and clean, protect cash and margin, and make the numbers usable for decisions.

**Reports to:** CEO
**Direct reports:** Accounting, FP&A, credit and collections, IT

**Owns**
- Monthly close, financial reporting, and the audit/review relationship
- Budget, forecast, and the annual operating plan mechanics
- Treasury, banking, debt covenants, and cash forecasting
- Credit, collections, and DSO
- Cost model, standard costs, margin floors, and price-list governance
- Capital request process: discipline, hurdle rates, post-investment review
- Plant-level and product-level profitability reporting
- IT systems and the ERP/MIS roadmap
- M&A diligence and purchase accounting support
- Insurance and risk

**Measures**
- EBITDA vs. plan
- Days to close; forecast accuracy vs. actual
- DSO and working capital
- Covenant compliance and liquidity headroom
- Clean audit / no material control findings
- Margin governance: realization reporting and exception discipline

# Compensation Plans

## Benchmark basis

Two real sources anchor these numbers.

**Chief Executive Research, *CEO and Senior Executive Compensation in Private Companies 2024-25*** — 1,534 US private companies surveyed March–July 2024, reporting FY2023 actuals. Medians across all private companies surveyed:

| Role | Median base | Median bonus | Bonus % of base | Median total comp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CEO (2024 expected cash) | $325,000 | $100,000 | ~31% | $425,000 cash |
| President | $259,200 | $79,000 | ~30% | $364,275 |
| COO | $218,000 | $42,000 | ~19% | $286,075 |
| CFO | $207,500 | $40,000 | ~19% | $273,575 |
| Chief Sales Officer | $192,250 | $50,250 | ~26% | $268,575 |

CEO pay scales sharply with revenue: median total compensation was $315,500 at $10–24.9M revenue and $608,364 at $100–249.9M. A $50M company interpolates to roughly $400–475K total.

**Ennis, Inc. (NYSE: EBF) proxy** — the structural template from a public business-forms company selling through distributors. CEO target bonus 125% of base with a 250% cap; other named executives target 50% with a 100% cap. Bonuses turn on pre-set company financial targets plus individual/department goals. Their long-term plan ties 60% of awards to EBITDA and ROE with a peer-group TSR modifier.

**Two adjustments applied below.** The survey medians are all-industry and skew to companies larger than $50M; commercial printing is a mature, low-margin sector that pays below the all-industry median. So the ranges sit at or modestly below survey median. Ennis's *target percentages* are public-company norms and are not used — but their target-with-a-defined-cap discipline is.

## Recommended ranges

| Role | Base salary | Target bonus (% of base) | Max bonus | Target total cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CEO | $275,000–350,000 | 40% | 80% | $385,000–490,000 |
| VP Operations | $190,000–240,000 | 30% | 60% | $247,000–312,000 |
| VP Sales | $170,000–215,000 | 35% | 70% | $230,000–290,000 |
| VP Finance | $180,000–225,000 | 25% | 50% | $225,000–281,000 |

Position within range by experience, prior scale, and geography. Long-term incentive is separate and addressed below.

## Bonus scorecards

Few measures, each one the person can move.

**CEO — target 40% of base**

| Measure | Weight |
|---|---|
| EBITDA vs. plan | 50% |
| Revenue growth, label & packaging broken out | 25% |
| Cash conversion / ROIC | 15% |
| Strategic milestones — integration, leadership bench | 10% |

**VP of Sales — target 35% of base**

| Measure | Weight |
|---|---|
| Revenue vs. plan | 40% |
| Gross margin dollars / price realization vs. floor | 30% |
| Label & packaging revenue growth vs. plan | 20% |
| Retention and new account acquisition | 10% |

Margin realization is weighted heavily on purpose: it stops the plan from rewarding revenue bought with price.

**VP of Operations — target 30% of base**

| Measure | Weight |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing operating performance — throughput, lead time, on-time delivery | 40% |
| Label & packaging capacity and capability milestones | 25% |
| EBITDA vs. plan | 20% |
| Safety, waste/quality, Lean deployment, GM bench | 15% |

**VP of Finance — target 25% of base**

| Measure | Weight |
|---|---|
| EBITDA vs. plan | 40% |
| Cash — DSO and working capital | 25% |
| Close timeliness, forecast accuracy, clean controls | 20% |
| Margin and capital governance | 15% |

## Long-term incentive

About half of US private companies have no formal long-term plan, which is the most common reason a good operator leaves for the acquirer down the road. Recommended: a three-year phantom equity or cash-settled appreciation plan for all four roles, sized at roughly 0.5–2.0% of equity value for the VPs and 3–8% for the CEO, vesting on a rolling three-year cycle and settling on a defined valuation formula or on a liquidity event.

Tie 60% to cumulative EBITDA and return on invested capital, per the Ennis structure. If phantom equity is unworkable, a three-year cash LTI pool funded on cumulative EBITDA above a threshold accomplishes most of it.

## Plan design notes

1. **Every role carries EBITDA.** Weighted differently, but nobody optimizes their function at the company's expense.
2. **Nobody is paid on what they don't control.** Sales is not paid on makeready; Operations is not paid on price realization.
3. **Target and cap, always.** Uncapped plans at this size create surprises in a good year and disputes in a bad one.
4. **Baselines get set in writing before the year starts** — especially throughput and revenue per manufacturing day, which are meaningless without a pre-agreed baseline.
5. **Buy the survey before finalizing.** These ranges are defensible for a first draft. For an offer, the full Chief Executive report segments by revenue tier, region, industry, and ownership type, and PRINTING United Alliance runs a printing-specific survey. Either gets you a number you can defend to a candidate or a board.
